# How it works

## Data
- `pipeline/` (Python) reads dated NASA SVS frames, takes a small area around each agreed place, and matches the
  colours to the image's colour key. Each disc is 12 monthly levels (rain: −4…+4 compared with normal; greenness: 1…4),
  a note on missing months, and the source frames used. Source hashes, frame counts and colour-match rates are kept in
  `pipeline/evidence/`; limits are in `pipeline/HANDOFF.md`.
- `docs/data/discs.json` holds 32 rain/greenness discs (8 places × two datasets × two years), plus two Tromso snow records with numerical values withheld. The GIBS images cannot distinguish transparent zero snow from missing observations or water; `pipeline/snow.py` never substitutes zero for those pixels. Raw MOD10CM values and quality flags are needed to restore quantitative snow playback. `discs.sample.json` is
  hand-made test data with a desert and a snow place (`?sample` in the address).

## Player and dialogs
- The console shows a condition mark on each disc (colour and shape), five real buttons and a green LCD line that reads out the month being played.
- The Details dialog (button or the I key) lists the 12-month chart and table, the NASA image of the disc, the piano roll, the dataset label and unit, its limits, colour match, frames used, the NASA SVS link and CREDITS.md; Esc closes it and focus goes back to where it was.
- How to use (button, the H key, and once by itself on the first visit) is a guided tour in the style of the team's Delphi game: the page dims, a gold-edged spotlight shows one button at a time (disc tray, Play, Stop, Flip, Mode, Eject, tray pages, Details, Settings) and a small card types one sentence about it (`docs/js/coach.js`). Two steps wait for the visitor to do it (put a disc on the deck, press Play); clicks go through the spotlight to the real control. SKIP ends it.
- Settings (High contrast, Reduce motion, Preview on focus, Volume) live in a dialog and are saved in this browser; contrast ratios of the colour pairs are checked by `node --test`.

## Pixel console
- The record player is a 480x270 pixel canvas scaled inside a scrollable page, with labelled controls below it: a night sky with a dot-matrix world map (the ping sits on the place and takes the colour of the month playing), weather that follows the landscape (rain, sand, falling snow that piles on the lid), and a wooden console with a tray of discs and a slate deck. `docs/js/scene.js` paints it; every button is a real HTML button laid over the painting, and all state changes go through the store.
- The tray reads in columns: rain on top, greenness below. The main page holds a river place, the Amazon, a desert place and a snow place; the small page button in the bottom-right corner of the tray (1/3 ▶) shows the other pages. The centre of each disc is painted for its landscape (river delta, city, wetland, lake, coast, forest, desert, ice).
- While a disc is on the deck, the tray becomes a **map screen**: the real NASA image of the month playing (the same SVS or GIBS image the data is read from, cut around the place, `tools/make_month_maps.py`), with the place marked by a small gold square (about the half-degree area the music reads) and the image's date and source under it. It crossfades month to month, so you see the rain spread, the land green up or the snow go. At the end of December the music stops, the needle lifts and the disc goes back on the shelf.
- While the music plays, the sky over the world map becomes a little pixel calendar of its 12 months, so the data can be read without sound. Rain: a wetter month is a rain cloud (bigger and with more drops and a puddle the wetter it is), a drier month a sun over cracked ground (bigger the drier), a near-normal month a small white cloud. Greenness: a sprout, bush, tree or big tree. The snow visualization is retained in code, but withheld snow records have no numerical snow glyphs. The two years of a place are two rows (named in the title with the NASA SVS page); the disc on the deck is the bright row. A playhead moves with the music and the playing month is lit. The year's peak month (the wettest, or the greenest) has a gold star and a gold name; for rain it is only marked when the peak is wetter than normal, and not when more than 3 months share the top.
- The glass tube in the slot between the tray and the deck is a rain gauge (blue) or a sap gauge (green): it fills and empties with the month playing (half full is a normal month for rain), and a gold high-water mark, like a flood mark on a wall, shows the year's peak; it flashes when the music reaches it.
- Enter or a click records a disc and puts it on the deck. The 12 lights on the deck are one row, or two (side A above side B) with a comparison that counts only usable pairs, such as "7 of 10 comparable months wetter in 2026 (2 months unavailable)". Flipping years preserves the calendar position.
- The first time a disc of a place is loaded, a story card covers most of the picture: pixel art of the place's landscape (made from a free nature photograph with `tools/make_pixel_art.py`, credited in CREDITS.md) with its name and story. It starts fading as soon as it appears (gone after about 4 seconds, or within about a second of pressing Play) (with Reduce motion it just shows for a moment and goes). A place without a picture has no card.
- The first click or key press starts audio (no start button). Turntable sounds are synthesized in `docs/js/foley.js`, separate from the music engine.

## Music
- `docs/js/schedule.js` turns a disc into notes, one bar (2 s) per month. A rain level k gives 4 + k data notes (0 to 8): up to four are the tune, and any extra in a wet month are short unpitched raindrop ticks. A greenness level q gives q layers in the chord (root, third, fifth, root) plus a steady pulse. A missing month is crackle only; a break in the source makes the record skip (`breaks` holds months 1–12).
- Harmony follows the basics: chords are plain major or minor triads in one register, the tune only uses notes of its month's chord and moves by small steps (never more than a fourth within a month), each month starts near where the last one ended, and December comes home to the tonic chord. The bass is one connected line (root, then a passing fifth into the next root). `node tools/note_audit.mjs` measures leaps and clashing notes on the real discs.
- Dry months sit lower and wet months higher, with fewer or more drops, and the background sound changes with the month too, so the state is heard in several ways at once.
- `docs/js/audio.js` plays the notes with the Web Audio API (no library) in a lofi style, with its own key, instruments and groove per landscape: Southeast Asia (plucked string, harp-like chords, hand drums), South Asia (flute, harmonium, tabla-like cycle over a continuous drone), desert (ney, oud, frame drum), snow and ice (bells, airy pad, almost no beat), elsewhere (electric piano, lofi kit).
- Under the music runs a quiet bed of real field recordings that follows the month (`docs/js/ambience.js`): wind, light and heavy rain, birds (Cambodian doves and tailorbirds recorded in Siem Reap for Tonle Sap and Hue, gentle park birds elsewhere). Birds are never a loop: they are an endless bed of 8-second stretches of the recording, each from a random spot, overlapping by half with sine and cosine fades (an equal-power crossfade, as audio editors use, so the loudness stays even with no seams), each slightly changed in speed and level; the bed is louder the greener the month (or the less snow), gliding over a few seconds, and even the busiest month is only a moderate background; in a desert a gusting open wind, and in snow places a hill wind with a howl in snowier months. It is faint in a normal month and swells the further a month is from normal.
- Round trip: reading the levels back from the notes gives back all 408 stored monthly slots, including missing slots; in science mode NDVI comes back within 0.020 (limit 1/48). `node --test` checks this.

## Seasonal comparisons
- The compact header leaves more room for the record player. The console fits both the container width and the viewport height, leaving 16 pixels below it at the top of the page.
- Resizing the window or loading a font recalculates that fit. Scrolling does not change its height budget; labelled controls and comparisons remain below it in the scrollable page.
- Pixelify Sans is used for the pixel interface, headings and buttons. Explanations and form labels use the visitor's system font, as recorded in `CREDITS.md`.
- `seasonal.js` aligns January to December for two years or two places. A then B takes two seconds per month; changes-only keeps unequal usable pairs. Nulls and flagged breaks never become a zero or count as comparable.
- Rain uses MIDI 60 + 2 × category and greenness uses MIDI 60 + 4 × (category − 1), consistently across every place and year. Duets require one place and a shared year, and play rain and greenness together with independent switches.
- `seasonal-audio.js` schedules notes on a shared Web Audio clock and renders the same plan to WAV. Starting either player stops the other. Month cards, keyboard navigation and text explanations work without sound and on portrait phones.

## Monthly evidence and Ferlo
- `tools/build_seasonal_evidence.mjs` checks monthly levels and frame counts against the pipeline evidence, then publishes dated source URLs, hashes and colour-decoding checks for all 32 records. Re-run it after rebuilding the data.
- Ferlo is sampled at 15.6035° N, 15.2857° W; all twelve 2025 greenness months are usable. It is an image-region sample, not an address-level measurement. Missing rain months remain missing, and transparent rain pixels can represent near normal.
- The inspector shows sources for the selected month; the July crop is explicitly labelled as context, not a monthly aggregate. Downloaded JSON contains the audition plan, track switches, volume, stored data and available evidence. Colour-match percentages describe decoding quality, not physical accuracy.
- Ferlo has 46 dated monthly map crops: 12 rain images for 2025, 10 for 2026, and 12 greenness images for each of 2024 and 2025. `python tools/make_month_maps.py --place ferlo` refreshes only those entries and preserves the other places.
- Each monthly map is a dated source image near the 15th, not an average of the frames used for the monthly category. Unavailable rain months have no substitute images.
- Tromso snow images remain in the source archive for visual context. All 24 numerical slots are withheld; the pipeline, published-data tests and browser validator prevent ambiguous transparency from becoming a snow percentage.
- After validating the full JSON, the app excludes explicitly withheld records from its shared playable catalog. Neither tray pages, keyboard shortcuts nor labelled controls can select the two empty snow discs; records with ordinary missing months remain available.
- Tromso's greenness disc stays on the front tray. The original 34 archived records and all 408 monthly slots remain available for source review and round-trip checks; 32 records are playable.
