# How we used SI (Super Intelligence / AI)

We used **Claude** (Anthropic, through Claude Code) as a coding and writing assistant, and **OpenAI Codex** for the data pipeline and the seasonal comparison updates. This document records that assistance; the product is presented under the team's name.

- **Where:** Claude wrote most of the original JavaScript in `docs/js/` (the page, the pixel scene, the music engine and its tests), the sound-cutting script and the note-checking script in `tools/`, and the notes and documents. OpenAI Codex helped Sabik write and test the Python data pipeline in `pipeline/` that reads colours from NASA SVS frames, and implemented the labelled controls, seasonal comparisons, duet auditions, audio exports and month-level evidence inspector at Sabik's request. Claude wrote `pipeline/snow.py` (snow cover for Tromso from NASA GIBS), on Ky's request, reusing the pipeline's checks; this update preserves those records.
- **What we did ourselves:** Bormey chose the four river places and wrote their introductions and sources. Ky gave the sampling coordinates, decided the look (a pixel jukebox, approved from art samples before it was built), chose the final sound by listening to six versions side by side, decided to add a forest, a desert and a snow place, and asked for the sky calendar, the story cards with pixel art, the snow cover data and the guided tour, judging each by eye and ear; Claude proposed the exact spots (Amazon near Manaus, Alice Springs, Tromso) after scanning the NASA frames, and Ky approved them. Sabik built, ran and checked the pipeline and found and fixed a longitude mistake in the rain frames. Yến set the scope and the story. Every sound complaint that shaped the music (clashing notes, too many notes, crackle, repeated loops) came from people listening.
- **Checks:** automated tests cover the pipeline, player, comparisons and WAV encoding. The music round trip reproduces all 408 stored monthly slots, including missing slots. The team checked the page by hand in a laptop browser and with the keyboard; browser control and responsive-layout checks were also assisted by Codex. Testing with a screen reader and on many physical phones was limited in the 48 hours, and the seasonal comparison controls still need those checks by the team.
- **The idea:** the idea is the team's own: the jukebox that plays Earth data as records, with Side A/B and a "scratched disc" for missing data, is in a team idea note dated 6 Nov, and Yến cut the scope on 9 Nov. Claude did not come up with the idea; it helped turn the team's decisions into code and documents.
- **Before the event (Nov 5–13):** we used Claude to study the NASA frames and to write design notes (how to read the colour bars, the data format, the music rules, the tests), one worked example with an illustration, prompts, and a generic accessible page template. Apart from that template, all code, data processing and sound in the product were made on Nov 14–15.
- **Not used:** no AI-generated images, video, voices or sound samples in the product. An AI-made mockup was used only as a look-and-feel reference before the event; the disc art and the console are painted by our code. The music is synthesized live by our code; the background sounds are real field recordings under free licences (CC0 and CC BY-SA), and the story-card pictures are pixel art our code made from real free photographs (see `CREDITS.md`). The one illustration in `notes/` has a visible SI label.
- **Marked in metadata:** `provenance` in `docs/data/discs.json`; commits made through Claude Code keep their `Co-Authored-By: Claude` line.
